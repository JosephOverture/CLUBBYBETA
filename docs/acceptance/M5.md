# M5 — tables and deposits review

Local engineering gate, 2026-09-23. M4 was completed and reviewed before this phase. Migration `005_table_reservations.sql` has now been applied to the persistent development database and must remain unchanged.

Implemented W14 with extensions to inventory, payments, admissions, refunds, journals, notifications, privacy and both clients. Physical tables have stable venue ownership; immutable versioned floor plans contain zones, geometry, guest capacity and rotation. Occurrences pin layouts. Immutable offers define exact slots, full package/deposit/minimum-spend/inclusion terms, guest limits, tax inputs and explicit no-show treatment. Audited publication changes and staff blocks preserve existing purchases. PostgreSQL's GiST exclusion constraint prevents overlapping physical table allocations, including cross-occurrence holds and staff blocks.

All guests consume shared occurrence capacity, receive distinct opaque credentials after payment, and require the remaining contracted balance before admission. Full and deposit bookings use the existing authoritative Stripe attempt and recovery workflow. Initial collection covers the full-package commission. Holds expire at the earlier of ten minutes or slot start and remain allocated when provider outcome is uncertain. Purchased layout and terms cannot change. The floor-plan/slot picker, package terms, shared payment UI, booking balances, full-party refund action, staff collection/correction view and scanner lookup are present in the web/iOS development client.

Door receipts are scoped, bounded by the outstanding balance, idempotent and immutable. Venue/method/reference uniqueness prevents counting a terminal receipt twice across bookings. Managers reverse records rather than edit them; all entries balance and keep provider cash separate from venue cash. Whole-party online refunds track separate door-money refund obligations. No-show marking requires its purchased grace period and no admissions; it retains paid amounts and waives uncollected debt. Later corrections do not recreate waived debt. Event cancellation or rescheduling immediately disables table sales and rights, and durably refunds the original appointment instead of silently moving it into another slot.

Review corrections included a cross-venue physical-ID insertion race, duplicate external receipt protection, negative refund obligations, waived-debt corrections, event-change integration, slot-based hold expiry, timezone display and migrated historical admission counts. Privacy exports include reservation and door receipt data. The package-price/minimum-spend interpretation and staff procedures are documented in `docs/architecture/table-model.md` and `docs/runbooks/tables.md`.

Evidence: `pnpm check` passes both typechecks, **87 tests across eight files**, and backend/worker builds. New real-PostgreSQL scenarios cover concurrent exclusive slots, the database exclusion itself, product-ID bypass, party capacity, deposits/commission and credentials, balance-required scans, receipt replay/overpayment/reversal, whole-party refunds and door refunds, layout snapshots, cancellation/expiry, no-show debt, scoped access, immutable finances, withdrawn offers, reschedule compensation, staff blocks and a populated M4-to-M5 schema upgrade. Both web and iOS/Hermes exports pass. Production dependency audit has zero advisories. Persistent local API/worker/PostgreSQL startup passes after migration.

Conclusion: M5 is locally complete; M6 may extend the same physical allocation and financial boundaries for group contributions. Real Stripe-hosted payments, venue terminal operations, signed device scenarios and production accounts remain external release evidence under the user's local preparation scope. Final seller/admin product screens, whole-app integration and release checks remain in M7/M8 as planned.
